## How the decoding was actually found

The legacy corpus preserves the final mechanical ledger more reliably than it preserves the discovery history.

The best reconstruction is that reversal, number-letter mapping, phonetic resemblance, semantic coherence, block boundaries, and block-specific operations were explored together. Linguistic and symbolic cues helped reveal where blocks ended and what roles they might have.

Only after the structure had emerged was the ledger stabilized so that another reader could reproduce the same output.

Therefore:

```text
reproducible ex post != preregistered discovery
```

## Why this matters

The digits themselves are fixed. The stabilized result can be reproduced. But the route by which the procedure was found contained human exploration and interpretation.

This should be documented rather than hidden, because the first serious validation question is not whether the discovery can be rewritten as a laboratory preregistration. It is whether the recovered structure has independent depth.

The next step is therefore comparative structural convergence with established literature, followed by domain-specific tests where the theory makes empirical claims.
