# Mathematical Foundations

## The ideal circle

Pi Theory begins from the ideal Euclidean circle. Its relevant properties include exact rotational symmetry, equal radii, no privileged angular direction, and a tangent at every circumference point.

## The defining relation

```text
pi = C / d
```

`C` is circumference. `d` is diameter.

Pi is dimensionless because the units cancel.

## Straight and circular measure

The diameter is straight; the circumference is circular and closed. Pi gives the invariant conversion between those two measures.

## Exact but inexhaustibly represented

Pi is irrational. Therefore a positional expansion of pi is non-terminating and non-repeating.

Pi itself is nevertheless exact. The number is not generated digit by digit; the digits are one representation of an already defined object.

## Important guardrail

The continuum of radii and tangents does not mathematically cause pi's irrationality. Geometric infinity and arithmetic irrationality are distinct properties.
