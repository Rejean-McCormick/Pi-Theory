# Language, Culture, and the Attunement Hypothesis

## 1. The problem

The decoding contains forms that can be interpreted through human language, even though human languages arose very late in cosmic history.

Pi Theory does not solve this by claiming that French or English literally existed before humans.

## 2. Stronger hypothesis

The current proposal is:

```text
common deep structure
-> physical constraints
-> biological constraints
-> cognition
-> language and culture
```

Human languages may crystallize recurring distinctions because speakers are embodied organisms formed by the same world they describe.

## 3. Three layers

A disciplined model separates:

1. deep functional structures such as cohesion, difference, containment, rupture, magnitude, and return;
2. embodied cognitive and articulatory constraints;
3. historically contingent languages such as French, Latin, English, or Chinese.

## 4. Naturalistic mechanisms are not competitors by default

Examples:

- `mama`-type forms may be common partly because bilabial sounds are easy for infants;
- `boom`-type forms are partly onomatopoeic;
- `bien`, `bon`, `bene`, and related forms are historically connected rather than independent confirmations.

Such mechanisms may explain how recurrent structures become linguistic forms. They cannot be counted as independent evidence for pi.

## 5. Testable version

A stronger scientific hypothesis would predict semantic or phonetic attractors across languages under preregistered criteria and compare them with null expectations from phonology, embodiment, and historical transmission.
