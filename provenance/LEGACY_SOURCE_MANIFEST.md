# Legacy Source Manifest

The public repository does not include all historical binary manuscripts. They remain research provenance, not canonical theory.

## Use policy

Legacy documents may be consulted for:

- recovery of exact decoding operations;
- discovery chronology;
- older formulations worth reconsidering;
- comparison with the current theory.

They must not be treated as authoritative simply because they are polished manuscripts. Many were AI-assisted and can contain invented certainty, weak citations, or retrospective methodological claims.

## Methodological correction

Several legacy papers state that the original decoding rules were fixed a priori or before meaningful outputs were seen. This is not the accepted history of the project.

The accepted sequence is:

```text
exploration -> structure found -> rules stabilized -> later testing
```

The manuscript commonly referred to as `Pi Theory with maths` is useful for the stabilized mechanical ledger, but its a-priori language is not accepted as a reliable account of discovery provenance.

Pi v13 is useful for another reason: it preserves traces that phonetic and semantic cues helped identify block limits and functions. That is consistent with an exploratory discovery process.

## Current authority

When a legacy statement conflicts with the active English core, the active core governs unless the conflict triggers a deliberate theory revision.
