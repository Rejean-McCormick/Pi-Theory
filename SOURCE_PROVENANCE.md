# Source Provenance

Pi Theory developed through iterative conversation, private notes, exploratory decoding, and AI-assisted drafting.

Legacy documents are not authoritative. They may contain useful expressions of the author's ideas and exact traces of the stabilized decoding, but they also contain AI extrapolations, unsupported probability claims, overextended analogies, occasional factual errors, and retrospective rewrites of methodology.

A particularly important correction concerns discovery history. Some legacy manuscripts describe the decoding pipeline as fixed a priori. That is rejected. The actual discovery was exploratory. The final ledger was stabilized after meaningful structure had been found.

Legacy texts are therefore used selectively:

- for exact digit strings and transformation records when independently checked;
- for evidence of the author's evolving concepts;
- for clues about how the decoding was discovered;
- never as automatic authority for historical, mathematical, scientific, or methodological claims.

The active English repository is a reconstruction of the strongest current theory, not a preservation of every historical statement.
